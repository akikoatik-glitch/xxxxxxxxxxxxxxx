Football prediction methodology — Dixon-Coles + Elo

By XWhiz Data Team · Updated 2026-09-04 · Model Dixon-Coles v3

XWhiz publishes football predictions from a quantitative model, not from intuition. Below is a transparent description of how each prediction is generated — what data we use, what the maths does, and where the model is intentionally limited.

1. Data sources

Every fixture comes from public sports-data feeds (football-data.org, openfootball/football.json, and additional public providers). We use:

  • Real fixtures for the next 7 days and recent results — never invented matches.
  • League tables (attack and defence per game, points per game).
  • Elo-style ratings updated after each completed match.

2. Team strength (Elo + league form)

Each team has an Elo rating representing its overall strength. For a new match we blend this with current-season league form: how many goals the team scores and concedes per game relative to the league average. The blend gives an attack and defence strength for both sides that is anchored in real data but adapts to recent form.

3. Expected goals via Poisson

We assume goals follow a Poisson process. Given the home team's attack strength λH and the away team's defence strength, expected home goals μH = λH × δA. Expected away goals μA = λA × δH. League-average goal rate is the anchor (around 1.3–1.5 per team per match).

4. Dixon-Coles low-score correction

The plain Poisson underestimates the rate of low-scoring draws (0-0, 1-0, 0-1, 1-1). We apply the Dixon-Coles correction τ(x, y, ρ) which depends on a single correlation parameter ρ < 0. This is the standard fix proposed by Dixon & Coles (1997). After correction the joint probability P(X = i, Y = j) is computed for the full 0–6 goal grid.

5. The score matrix

The model produces a 7×7 probability matrix where cell (i, j) is the chance of a home i – away j final score. From this matrix every other market is derived — there are no separate formulas for Over/Under or BTTS.

6. Market probabilities

  • 1X2. Home = Σ over (i > j). Draw = Σ over (i = j). Away = Σ over (i < j).
  • Over/Under 2.5. Sum of cell probabilities where i + j ≥ 3 (Over) or i + j ≤ 2 (Under).
  • BTTS. Yes = Σ over (i ≥ 1 and j ≥ 1). No = 1 − P(BTTS Yes).
  • Correct score. The cell with the highest probability; we list the top three most probable scorelines.

7. Fair odds vs bookmaker odds

All odds shown on XWhiz are fair odds derived directly from the model: fair odds = 100 ÷ probability%. They are not bookmaker prices and do not include the bookmaker margin. If you compare our odds to a sportsbook you should expect our numbers to be lower (because real prices contain overround). We never present model output as a bookmaker quote.

8. Confidence and value

Confidence is simply the model's top-probability pick (e.g. 82% on Away Win means P(Away) = 0.82). Value is the model's edge versus a typical bookmaker line and is provided for context only. Higher confidence is not the same as a guaranteed outcome.

9. Refresh schedule

Predictions regenerate once per day at 06:00 UTC. The model is deterministic for a given input set, so refresh frequency is bounded by the upstream data feed.

10. What the model does not account for

Limitations. The model is intentionally narrow. It does not include: late team-news (injuries, suspensions, rotation), tactical match-ups, manager changes, weather, derby motivation, competition stage pressure, travel fatigue, red-card response, or in-game momentum. A prediction is a probability estimate, not a forecast of a specific event.

11. Historical performance

Once a sufficient number of results are stored against the model's published probabilities, we will publish historical performance metrics (accuracy, Brier score, calibration, simulated ROI) on a dedicated transparency page. Until then we deliberately do not publish headline accuracy numbers — accuracy on small samples is noisy and can mislead.

12. Editorial policy

Editorial decisions (which picks to feature, which competitions to cover, how to label markets) are independent of the model's output. Affiliate relationships do not and cannot influence what the model produces or which matches are published.
